## 1. Canonical manuscript-level framework claim

The single canonical manuscript claim is:

> **A preregistered physics-informed finite-time workflow can test whether a conventional positive storage/state objective is redundant with an independently defined signed physical channel on the same admissible perturbation space; across the three frozen models considered here, that nonredundancy is strong in the Plasma and Neuro pilots but weak in the Climate/Ocean pilot, showing that optimizer geometry and practical channel-performance loss must be assessed separately.**

Equivalent shorter formulation for Abstract/Conclusion:

> **Storage/state-optimal and physical-channel-optimal perturbations need not be redundant, but the magnitude and practical consequence of their separation are system-, horizon-, observable- and admissible-geometry-dependent.**

This claim is **not** a theorem of universality. It is an integrated, preregistered application/methodology claim supported by three frozen case studies.

### N2 — methodological integration

The contribution is the disciplined combination of

\[
\mathfrak C=(A,M,Q,B,R_{\rm in}),
\]

physical predefinition of `M`, `Q` and admissible `B`, finite-time positive and signed-channel operators, optimizer/subspace comparison, signed extrema, performance-gap diagnostics, direct physical reconstruction, preregistered robustness rules, and anti-retuning freezes.

### N3 — physical insight

The integrated physical insight is twofold:

1. a conventional positive storage/state optimum can be a poor proxy for a separately defined physical channel optimum in some admissible geometries;
2. a large optimizer/subspace angle can coexist with a small target-performance penalty, so geometric separation alone is not sufficient evidence of practical objective nonredundancy.

No mathematical novelty is claimed for:

- quadratic-output finite-time optimization;

- singular vectors or optimal perturbations;
- the generic fact that different objectives may have different optimizers;
- adjoint/Gramian/Lyapunov methods;

The manuscript must not be marketed as a new general theory of these objects.

## 3. Semantic invariants across domains

The commonality is mathematical/methodological, not physical. The manuscript must preserve the following distinctions everywhere, including figure labels and captions.

| Domain | Positive metric/objective | Signed physical channel | Admissible geometry / cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plasma/D10-ZF | free energy | radial particle transport | frozen perturbation state space, `B=I`, `R_in=M` |
| Neuro/CMC | model-internal synaptic-filter storage at terminal time | contribution of the predefined V1-SP -> V4-SS pathway to the storage-rate balance | fixed rank-two two-pulse afferent preparation, `R_in=I_2` in pulse-amplitude coordinates |
| Climate/Ocean QG | QG perturbation energy | signed cumulative meridional eddy heat transport | balanced QG eddy state space, `B=I`, `R_in=M_K` |

In particular:

- Neuro `M` is **not** metabolic brain energy, thermodynamic brain energy or total physiological energy.
- Neuro `G_M` is terminal synaptic-filter storage per frozen input cost; it is not automatically an initial-storage gain.
- Climate and Plasma use genuine energy/free-energy metrics, but their physical meanings differ.
- `Q` is a separately defined physical channel, not a generic positive norm.

## 4. Allowed and forbidden claim matrix

### 4.1 Plasma/D10-ZF — strong primary domain anchor

**Allowed claims**

- The frozen D10-ZF point is spectrally stable, `alpha(A)<0`, while finite-time free-energy amplification exceeds unity over the reported horizon ladder.
- The physically defined signed cumulative particle-transport operator has distinct positive and negative extrema.
- Energy-optimal and particle-transport-optimal initial perturbations are resolution robust and physically distinct.
- At frozen `T=1`,
  \[
  \Delta_\Gamma\simeq0.504,
  \]
  so the energy-optimal perturbation realizes only about one half of the maximum attainable positive cumulative particle transport.
- The result supports the hierarchy
  \[
  \text{modal stability}\neq\text{finite-time energy optimality}\neq\text{finite-time transport optimality}
  \]
  in this controlled benchmark.

**Forbidden / overclaim language**

- “first demonstration of transient growth in plasma”;
- “new quadratic-output mathematics”;
- “universal plasma theorem”;
- “proof that energy-optimal perturbations are generally transport-poor”;
- nonlinear turbulence or experimental validation claims from this linear benchmark.

**Required prior-art positioning**

The Plasma discussion must acknowledge at minimum:

- Landreman, Plunk & Dorland (2015), *Generalized universal instability: transient linear amplification and subcritical turbulence*, J. Plasma Phys. 81, 905810501, DOI `10.1017/S0022377815000495`, for transient amplification without unstable eigenmodes in plasma;
- Foures, Caulfield & Schmid (2014), *Optimal mixing in two-dimensional plane Poiseuille flow at finite Péclet number*, J. Fluid Mech. 748, 241–277, DOI `10.1017/jfm.2014.182`, as strong fluid-mechanical precedent that energy-optimal and mixing-optimal perturbations can differ;
- Sévellec et al. (2008), cited below under Climate, as adjacent precedent for observable-dependent ocean optimal perturbations.

The P2-A-specific integrated Plasma comparison remains a cautious application/methodology claim, not a broad first-principles novelty claim.

### 4.2 Neuro/CMC — strong cross-domain demonstrator

**Allowed claims**

- In the frozen stable two-source V1/V4 CMC/DCM tangent model, two independently defined finite-time objectives select different preparations within the same fixed rank-two two-pulse afferent coordinate space.
- The terminal synaptic-filter-storage optimum and the cumulative V1-SP -> V4-SS pathway-contribution optimum satisfy the preregistered strong thresholds at neighboring horizons 112 and 224 ms.
- At 112 ms,
  \[
  \vartheta=46.824271^\circ,\qquad \Delta_Q=0.529017,
  \]
  and at 224 ms,
  \[
  \vartheta=65.058256^\circ,\qquad \Delta_Q=0.817841.
  \]
- The distinction has direct preparation-space meaning: the pathway optimum uses approximately `(+0.9924,-0.1230)`, while the long-horizon terminal-storage optimum uses a same-sign pulse mixture, e.g. `(+0.5300,+0.8480)` at 224 ms.
- Safe novelty-sensitive wording is:
  > **To our knowledge, the targeted literature does not already contain the same stable CMC/DCM preparation problem in which terminal quadratic synaptic-filter storage and the cumulative contribution of a predefined physiological pathway are separately optimized over the same fixed rank-two two-pulse preparation coordinates.**

**Required restriction**

The full-state instantaneous pathway matrix is indefinite, but the frozen two-dimensional admissible preparation geometry does not realize a negative cumulative pathway extremum over the horizon ladder. Therefore the pilot does **not** demonstrate experimentally reachable positive and negative cumulative pathway transfer.

**Forbidden / overclaim language**

- “brain energy” or “metabolic energy” for `M`;
- “first optimal stimulation in DCM/CMC”;
- “first nonmodal/transient neural amplification”;
- “first optimized DCM experimental design”;
- “first pathway/effective-connectivity analysis”;
- claims of experimentally reachable negative cumulative pathway transfer;
- claims that the frozen linear CMC model establishes in-vivo causal efficacy.

**Required prior-art positioning**

The Introduction/Discussion should explicitly acknowledge the strongest neighboring literatures:

- Hennequin, Vogels & Gerstner (2012), *Non-normal amplification in random balanced neuronal networks*, Phys. Rev. E 86, 011909;
- Bondanelli & Ostojic (2020), *Coding with transient trajectories in recurrent neural networks*, PLoS Comput. Biol. 16, e1007655;
- Friston, Harrison & Penny (2003), *Dynamic causal modelling*, NeuroImage 19, 1273–1302;
- Daunizeau et al. (2011), *Optimizing Experimental Design for Comparing Models of Brain Function*, PLoS Comput. Biol. 7, e1002280;
- Salfenmoser & Obermayer (2022), *Nonlinear optimal control of a mean-field model of neural population dynamics*, Front. Comput. Neurosci. 16, 931121;
- Ogino et al. (2026), *Designing optimal perturbation inputs for system identification in neuroscience*, eLife reviewed preprint 110030.

These sources demote broad method novelty while leaving the frozen same-coordinate dual-objective comparison positionable with `to our knowledge` language.

### 4.3 Climate/Ocean QG — robust weak/contrast case

**Allowed claims**

- The frozen damped two-layer Phillips-QG pilot is spectrally stable and all structural/numerical/resolution gates pass.
- Energy-optimal and positive cumulative heat-transport-optimal perturbations are nearly coincident at the first five horizons.
- At `T/tau_ref=8`, they select different resolution-robust modal subspaces,
  \[
  (|m|,n)_E=(3,2),\qquad (|m|,n)_{\rm heat}=(4,2),
  \]
  with
  \[
  \vartheta_{\rm sub}=90^\circ,
  \]
  but only
  \[
  \Delta_{\rm heat}=0.04118455338.
  \]
- The energy optimum therefore retains about 95.88% of the maximum cumulative poleward heat transport.
- The principal methodological lesson is:
  \[
  \boxed{\text{large optimizer/subspace angle does not imply a large objective-performance gap}.}
  \]
- Safe novelty-sensitive wording is:
  > **To our knowledge, the targeted literature does not report the same direct energy-optimal versus signed cumulative eddy-heat-transport-optimal comparison in a stable damped two-layer Phillips-QG tangent system.**

**Forbidden / overclaim language**

- “strong Climate replication”;
- “energy-optimal perturbations fail to transport heat” as a generic claim;
- novelty claims for damped baroclinic transient growth, QG singular vectors or norm dependence;
- novelty claims for heat-transport-optimal perturbations in ocean/climate models generally;
- forecast-skill, blocking, AMOC or Primitive-Equation conclusions;
- any post-result retuning to create a stronger gap.

**Required prior-art positioning**

At minimum cite/discuss:

- Farrell (1982), *The Initial Growth of Disturbances in a Baroclinic Flow*, J. Atmos. Sci. 39, 1663–1686;
- Farrell (1985), *Transient Growth of Damped Baroclinic Waves*, J. Atmos. Sci. 42, 2718–2727;
- Farrell & Ioannou (1994), *A Theory for the Statistical Equilibrium Energy Spectrum and Heat Flux Produced by Transient Baroclinic Waves*, J. Atmos. Sci. 51, 2685–2698;
- Kim & Morgan (2002), *Dependence of Singular Vector Structure and Evolution on the Choice of Norm*, J. Atmos. Sci. 59, 3099–3116;
- Kuang (2004), *The Norm Dependence of Singular Vectors*, J. Atmos. Sci. 61, 2943–2949;
- Sévellec et al. (2008), *Optimal Surface Salinity Perturbations of the Meridional Overturning and Heat Transport in a Global Ocean General Circulation Model*, J. Phys. Oceanogr. 38, DOI `10.1175/2008JPO3875.1`.

This dense prior art is a reason to keep Climate explicitly as a controlled contrast case rather than a headline novelty claim.

## 5. Section-level claim guardrails

### Abstract

The Abstract may state the common workflow, the nonuniform three-domain outcomes, one representative quantitative witness per domain, and the angle-versus-performance lesson.

It must not contain “new theory”, “universal”, “first transient growth”, “first optimal perturbation”, “brain energy”, or imply that Climate is strong.

Recommended Abstract-level sentence:

> **Using a preregistered common workflow, we find strong objective nonredundancy in a stable drift-wave plasma benchmark and in a constrained CMC/DCM preparation problem, but only weak performance nonredundancy in a stable two-layer QG heat-transport case, where orthogonal optimal subspaces still differ by only 4.12% in target performance.**

### Introduction

The Introduction must lead with the scientific problem of proxy objectives: a positive storage/state metric may be physically useful yet need not answer the same question as a signed channel of actual interest.

It must immediately acknowledge that transient growth, singular vectors, objective-dependent optimization and optimal control are established. The gap is the integrated, physics-predefined, preregistered comparison workflow and its cross-domain evidence.

### Results

Results should report each frozen verdict exactly as obtained. `CLIM-WEAK` is not to be softened, omitted or reframed as a hidden strong result. Optimizer angles must always be paired with a performance-gap quantity.

### Discussion

Discussion should emphasize:

1. objective nonredundancy is empirical/model-dependent, not guaranteed;
2. admissible geometry `B` is part of the physics, not a numerical convenience;
3. signed channels and positive storage metrics answer different physical questions;
4. the Climate result is a built-in falsification/contrast case for effect-maximizing interpretations;
5. model fidelity and experimental validation remain future work.

Preferred framing:

Do **not** frame the first paper as:

- a new general mathematical theory;

This ordering supports the central methodological message without flattening domain semantics.

### 2. Common finite-time framework and preregistration protocol

Define

\[
\mathfrak C=(A,M,Q,B,R_{\rm in}),
\]

\[
K_M(T)=R_{\rm in}^{-1/2}B^\dagger e^{A^\dagger T}Me^{AT}BR_{\rm in}^{-1/2},
\]

and

\[
K_Q(T)=R_{\rm in}^{-1/2}B^\dagger\left[\int_0^T e^{A^\dagger t}Qe^{At}\,dt\right]BR_{\rm in}^{-1/2}.
\]

Explain signed extrema, optimizer/subspace comparison, `Delta_Q`, resolution/trajectory checks, and anti-retuning rules. Present `H_j/nu` only if needed for completeness; they are not central to the first manuscript claim.

### 3. Plasma anchor: stable D10-ZF particle transport

- frozen model and physical channel;
- spectral stability plus transient free-energy amplification;
- positive/negative signed transport extrema;
- energy-versus-transport optimizer separation;
- quantitative witness `Delta_Gamma(T=1)≈0.504`;
- literature-safe interpretation.

### 4. Neuro strong cross-domain demonstrator

- frozen CMC/DCM architecture and synaptic-filter storage semantics;
- fixed V1-SP -> V4-SS channel;
- two-pulse admissible geometry;
- long-horizon strong separation and pulse-coordinate interpretation;
- restriction on negative cumulative reachability.

### 5. Climate/Ocean weak contrast case

- stable two-layer Phillips-QG setup;
- signed eddy heat channel and resolution protocol;
- nearly redundant shorter horizons;
- `90°` subspace switch at `T/tau_ref=8` with only `4.12%` performance gap;

### 6. Cross-domain synthesis

- compare what is shared and what is domain specific;
- show strong/strong/weak outcome hierarchy;

- explain role of admissible geometry.

### 7. Discussion and limitations

- prior-art positioning by domain;
- no mathematical novelty claim;
- model-fidelity limits;
- no post-hoc retuning;
- protected future branches.

### 8. Conclusion

Return to the canonical framework claim and emphasize that the workflow diagnoses redundancy rather than presupposing separation.

Detailed numerical assembly, resolution ladders and validation tables should go to Methods/Supplementary Material where journal format permits.
