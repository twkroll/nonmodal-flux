## Required current research

Use current official journal/publisher sources on the web. For each serious candidate, verify where available:

- journal scope and intended audience;
- relevant article type(s);
- whether a cross-domain methods/application paper of this type is in scope;

## Candidate evaluation

Evaluate a compact but meaningful candidate set (normally 5–8 journals) spanning the plausible audience options for the current manuscript, for example broad nonlinear/complex-systems, statistical/condensed-matter physics, and cross-disciplinary physical-science methods/application venues. Do not include a journal merely for prestige if the scope fit is poor.

For each candidate, assess:

1. scientific-scope fit;
2. audience fit for the Plasma–Neuro–Climate cross-domain story;
3. tolerance for a methodological integration contribution with `N2+N3` novelty rather than new mathematics;
4. fit of the strong/strong/weak plus robustness-rejection evidence pattern;
5. likely editorial risks, especially “too broad”, “too application-diverse”, “insufficient mathematical novelty”, or “insufficient domain depth”;
6. manuscript restructuring needed, if any, restricted to editorial changes only;
7. figure/table implications under the frozen scientific content;
8. title implications;
9. access/APC considerations, stated separately from scientific fit.

Classify candidates as:

- `FIT-A` — strong target fit;
- `FIT-B` — plausible with manageable editorial adaptation;
- `FIT-C` — possible but meaningful scope/audience risk;
- `DROP` — do not recommend.
