## 0. Frozen objects

The nominated neuro candidate remains exclusively

\[
\boxed{\text{multi-region CMC/DCM}}.
\]

The primary physiological channel remains

\[
\boxed{\mathrm{SP}_j\rightarrow\mathrm{SS}_i}
\]

with

\[
Q_{j\to i}^{\rm CORE}
=
\frac12\left(A_{j\to i}^{\dagger}M+MA_{j\to i}\right).
\]

The present gate does **not** alter `M`, `A_{j\to i}`, the signed-channel convention, or the cumulative objective definition. It asks only whether a physiologically and experimentally admissible initial-perturbation geometry can be fixed with

\[
\operatorname{rank}(B)\ge 2
\]

without using `B=I`, arbitrary hidden-state kicks, effect-guided input selection, or time-dependent optimal control.

A notation warning is essential: DCM itself conventionally uses `A/B/C` for endogenous connectivity, modulatory effects, and direct experimental inputs. In this document, **`B` is reserved for the CORE admissible initial-state map**. Direct DCM experimental inputs are denoted `C_DCM` or by physical injection vectors `b_aff`.

## 1. Pre-CORE facts about the CMC input structure

In convolution-based CMC/DCM, each population has two hidden synaptic-filter states, conveniently written as postsynaptic depolarization and its first derivative/current-like state. Experimental inputs are distinct from intrinsic and extrinsic cortical connections. In the standard CMC construction, external afferent input enters the spiny-stellate input population; the local synaptic filter is second order.

For one input-receiving spiny-stellate population, the passive part has the generic form

\[
\dot q=p,
\qquad
\dot p=-\kappa^2 q-2\kappa p+g\,u(t)+\cdots,
\]

where `q` is the postsynaptic depolarization perturbation, `p=\dot q`, `\kappa>0` is the synaptic rate constant, and `g\ne0` is the fixed input gain. Thus the instantaneous physiological injection direction is

\[
b_{\rm aff,j}
\propto
\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}
\]

in the local `(q,p)` block of `SS_j` and is zero on the other hidden states before propagation by the CMC dynamics.

This single instantaneous injection vector is rank one. The question is whether a **multi-component experimental preparation protocol** can generate a non-artificial reachable initial-state subspace of rank at least two while retaining this same physiological input pathway.

The answer is yes.

# 2. Option I — two fixed brief afferent stimulus components through the same physiological input pathway

## 2.1 Definition

Use the already accepted physical afferent pathway into `SS_j`, but prepare the state with two short stimulus components whose shapes and timings are fixed **before CORE**.

Let

\[
0<\tau_1<\tau_2
\]

be two pre-registered preparation delays relative to the start of the CORE observation window `t=0`. Let the two physical stimulus waveforms be fixed functions `h_1(t)` and `h_2(t)`, supported before `t=0`, with independently adjustable scalar amplitudes

\[
a=(a_1,a_2)^T.
\]

During the preparation window only,

\[
u(t)=a_1h_1(t)+a_2h_2(t).
\]

The waveforms, their timing, their modality, and their mapping to the DCM afferent input are frozen before any CORE calculation. There is no control input after `t=0`.

For the frozen linearized CMC generator `A`, the state at the start of the CORE window is

\[
x(0)=B_{\rm prep}a,
\]

with

\[
B_{\rm prep}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
 b_1^{\rm eff} & b_2^{\rm eff}
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
b_k^{\rm eff}
=
\int_{-\infty}^{0}
 e^{-As}b_{\rm aff,j}h_k(s)\,ds.
\]

This is a finite-dimensional reachable initial-state map generated by an explicit physiological input model. It is **not** a free hidden-state perturbation.

For idealized short impulse components at `t=-\tau_k`, this reduces to

\[
\boxed{
B_{\rm prep}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
 e^{A\tau_1}b_{\rm aff,j}
 &
 e^{A\tau_2}b_{\rm aff,j}
\end{bmatrix}.
}
\]

The ideal impulses are only a clean analytical representation of fixed brief experimental pulses; a later implementation should use the actual pre-registered finite pulse shape.

## 2.2 Rank: exact structural check in the synaptic-filter block

For the passive spiny-stellate second-order filter

\[
F=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
-\kappa^2&-2\kappa
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
b=g
\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix},
\]

the state produced at time `\tau>0` by a unit impulse into the physiological afferent channel is

\[
e^{F\tau}b
=
g e^{-\kappa\tau}
\begin{pmatrix}
\tau\\
1-\kappa\tau
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Therefore two distinct preparation delays give the local two-column matrix

\[
B_{\rm loc}
=
g
\begin{pmatrix}
\tau_1e^{-\kappa\tau_1}
&
\tau_2e^{-\kappa\tau_2}
\\
(1-\kappa\tau_1)e^{-\kappa\tau_1}
&
(1-\kappa\tau_2)e^{-\kappa\tau_2}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its determinant is

\[
\det B_{\rm loc}
=
g^2e^{-\kappa(\tau_1+\tau_2)}(\tau_1-\tau_2).
\]

Hence, for the physiologically required nonzero afferent gain and two distinct fixed delays,

\[
\boxed{
 g\ne0,
\quad
\tau_1\ne\tau_2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{rank}(B_{\rm loc})=2.
}
\]

This rank result is **not obtained by parameter search** and does not depend on any CORE objective. It is a consequence of the second-order synaptic-filter dynamics itself: an earlier and a later pulse produce different mixtures of postsynaptic displacement and current/velocity at the beginning of the analysis window.

In the full CMC network, the canonical admissible map is the full-system `B_prep` above. Its numerical rank should later be verified once the model point is frozen, but the local CMC input block already supplies a structural rank-two mechanism; no extra hidden-state channel is introduced.

## 2.3 Experimental meaning

The two coefficients `a_1,a_2` are amplitudes of two **separately controllable brief afferent stimulus components** delivered through the same experimentally defined sensory/afferent pathway to the source represented by `SS_j`.

Examples at the level of experimental design include two fixed sensory pulses of the same modality with a pre-registered inter-stimulus interval. The crucial requirements are not the specific sensory modality but:

1. both pulses use the same independently justified afferent CMC input pathway;
2. their timings and shapes are fixed before CORE;
3. their amplitudes can be manipulated independently by the experimenter;
4. the CORE observation window starts after the preparation pulses;
5. no control waveform is optimized during the observation window.

Thus the independent directions are experimentally accessible **input-component directions**, while the resulting distinct state-space directions arise naturally from the CMC dynamics.

## 2.4 Input metric `R_in`

Let the two unit-amplitude physical preparation waveforms be `h_1,h_2`. A pre-CORE experimental drive/dose metric is

\[
\boxed{
(R_{\rm in})_{k\ell}
=
\frac{1}{E_{\rm ref}}
\int h_k(t)h_\ell(t)\,dt,
}
\]

where `E_ref` is a fixed calibration/reference dose in the same experimental stimulus units.

For non-overlapping pulses of the same modality that are each normalized to the same unit dose,

\[
\boxed{R_{\rm in}=I_2.}
\]

Here `I_2` is justified **in input-coordinate space**, not in hidden-state space: the coordinates are two equally calibrated physical stimulus components. If the two pulse shapes have unequal physical dose or overlap, the corresponding positive Gram matrix is retained instead of whitening it for convenience.

This `R_in` is an experimental input/dose norm, not a claim about metabolic brain energy.

## 2.5 Does this become time-dependent optimal control?

No, provided the following freeze is respected:

- `h_1,h_2` fixed;
- `\tau_1,\tau_2` fixed;
- input pathway fixed;
- preparation window fixed;
- no input applied for `t\ge0`;
- no optimization over pulse timing, waveform, bandwidth, or continuous control trajectory.

The later CORE problem sees only

\[
x(0)=B_{\rm prep}a
\]

with a two-dimensional admissible coefficient vector. The temporal preparation is part of the **physical definition of the initial-state subspace**, not a new optimal-control problem.

## 2.6 Gate assessment

- `rank(B)`: **2**, structurally for distinct preparation delays in the second-order synaptic-filter block.
- experimental meaning: **strong**.
- independent accessibility: **yes**, through independently set pulse amplitudes.
- `R_in`: **strong**, pulse-energy/dose Gram matrix fixed before CORE.
- artificial hidden-state freedom: **none**.
- additional stimulation model required: **no**, beyond the already accepted CMC afferent input and a fixed pulse protocol.

\[
\boxed{\text{OPTION I: STRONG PASS}}
\]

# 3. Option II — two independently manipulated direct DCM driving inputs to distinct source regions

## 3.1 Definition

DCM explicitly permits one or more external experimental inputs and a direct-input matrix `C_DCM` specifying which sources receive them. In a multi-region CMC, distinct experimental inputs can therefore be assigned to spiny-stellate input populations of distinct pre-registered sources.

For two source regions `j` and `k`, define

\[
B_{\rm spatial}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
 b_{\rm aff,j} & b_{\rm aff,k}
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad j\ne k.
\]

Because the two injection vectors have support on different source-specific spiny-stellate state blocks,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(B_{\rm spatial})=2}
\]

provided both direct input gains are nonzero.

This is standard direct-input geometry, not a mathematical rank augmentation: DCM's experimental-input structure is part of the generative model and is specified from the experimental design.

## 3.2 Experimental meaning

The two columns must correspond to genuinely independently manipulated afferent stimulus streams. Scientifically clean examples are two spatially separated same-modality inputs or two separately controllable afferent streams whose receiving source regions are specified before model inversion.

The source assignments must not be chosen after inspecting any CORE quantity.

## 3.3 `R_in`

For two same-modality, equally calibrated stimulus channels with fixed unit pulse shapes,

\[
R_{\rm in}=I_2
\]

is again justified in experimental input coordinates. More generally,

\[
R_{\rm in}=\operatorname{diag}(E_1,E_2)/E_{\rm ref}
\]

or the full waveform Gram matrix should be used.

If the two inputs are from different modalities, relative weighting becomes less canonical because physical units differ. A cross-modal pilot would therefore require an independently specified calibration or dose normalization before CORE.

## 3.4 Independence

If the two stimulus streams can be varied separately and enter different source-specific `SS` populations, they are both experimentally and state-space independent.

## 3.5 Gate assessment

- `rank(B)`: **2** exactly for two distinct nonzero source injections.
- experimental meaning: **strong**.
- independent accessibility: **yes**, if the experiment has two independently manipulated direct inputs.
- `R_in`: **strong for same-modality calibrated channels; conditional for cross-modal channels**.
- weakness: requires a richer multi-input experimental architecture than the minimal single-afferent nomination.

\[
\boxed{\text{OPTION II: PASS}}
\]

Option II demonstrates that rank `\ge2` is not unique to the temporal-preparation construction. It is not chosen as the canonical first geometry because Option I preserves the originally nominated single physiological afferent pathway and requires fewer additional anatomical assumptions.

# 4. Option III — explicit multi-site electrical/TMS stimulation forward model

A third physically plausible route would define multiple independently actuated stimulation patterns, map each pattern through an electromagnetic/stimulation forward model to population-level CMC drives, and use the resulting columns as an admissible state/input map.

Schematically,

\[
B_{\rm stim}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
 b_{\rm stim,1}&\cdots&b_{\rm stim,m}
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

If at least two hardware patterns produce linearly independent population-drive vectors,

\[
\operatorname{rank}(B_{\rm stim})\ge2.
\]

A physical input metric could then be based on calibrated electrical current/field/coil-energy cost, for example a positive quadratic form

\[
a^TR_{\rm in}a
\]

fixed by the device and montage model.

However, the standard CMC/DCM equations do not by themselves determine how a particular TMS coil, electrode montage, or intracranial contact projects onto the four CMC populations. That mapping requires an explicit stimulation forward model and neurophysiological assumptions about population recruitment.

Therefore this route is **not needed** to rescue the first CMC pilot and should not be introduced merely to enlarge the admissible rank.

\[
\boxed{\text{OPTION III: CONDITIONAL / HOLD}}
\]

If pursued later, it would require a separate stimulation-model freeze before CORE.

# 5. Rejected rank-augmentation routes

The following are not admissible for Pilot 0.1:

### 5.1 `B=I`

Rejected. It would allow independent perturbations of latent depolarization/current states with no experimental or physiological actuator.

### 5.2 Independent kicks to arbitrary CMC populations

Rejected unless a separate experimental/stimulation model independently demonstrates access to those populations.

### 5.3 Treating DCM modulatory inputs as additive initial-state directions

Rejected. DCM modulatory inputs change coupling parameters and therefore define a different, time-dependent/operator-modulation problem rather than the present fixed-`A` initial-state geometry.

### 5.4 Choosing pulse times, source locations, or input columns after inspecting CORE effects

Rejected. The complete preparation protocol and input mapping must be frozen before any CORE calculation.

### 5.5 Optimizing the preparation waveform

Rejected for Pilot 0.1. That would move the project toward time-dependent optimal-control theory, which is outside this gate.

B

where both columns are generated by the **same already accepted afferent pathway into `SS_j`**, using two fixed brief stimulus components at distinct pre-registered preparation delays.

\[

# 7. Required preregistration before any later CORE execution

The present gate establishes the existence and canonical form of a natural rank-two admissible geometry. A subsequent pilot specification must freeze, without search for a large effect:

1. the exact CMC network and stable operating point;
2. the physical/source identity of `j` and `i`;
3. the exact afferent pathway into `SS_j`;
4. two fixed preparation pulse shapes `h_1,h_2`;
5. their fixed delays `\tau_1\ne\tau_2`;
6. the stimulus-to-DCM input calibration;
7. `R_in` from the corresponding fixed pulse-dose metric;
8. the numerical rank/conditioning of the resulting full-system `B_prep` as a qualification check only, not as a parameter-selection criterion.

If the full frozen model unexpectedly collapses the effective two-column map numerically to rank one, the pilot must stop rather than retune pulse timing to restore a desired CORE effect.

# 8. Literature basis used for this gate

The gate relies only on established pre-CORE model/input structure:

- Pinotsis DA et al., **Dynamic causal modelling of lateral interactions in the visual cortex**, NeuroImage (2013): CMC as a four-population convolution model; second-order postsynaptic filtering; external input mapped to the input/spiny-stellate population.
- Friston KJ et al., **Dynamic causal modelling revisited**, NeuroImage (2019): experimental inputs, intrinsic/extrinsic afferents, and population-specific hidden synaptic states in CMC/DCM.
- SPM documentation, **Dynamic Causal Modelling for M/EEG**: one or more experimental inputs can be specified and routed to model sources; direct inputs are distinct from forward/backward/lateral connectivity and from modulatory effects.
- SPM tutorial, **DCM for evoked responses**: direct sensory/subcortical input specification and source-specific input structure in evoked-response DCM.

No claim of CORE novelty or effect size is derived from these references.

# 9. Gate decision

A natural admissible perturbation geometry with rank at least two exists **without** `B=I`, without arbitrary hidden-state kicks, without effect-guided input selection, and without converting the pilot into time-dependent optimal control.

The strongest construction uses two independently amplitude-controlled, fixed brief afferent stimulus components through the same physiologically accepted `SS_j` input pathway. The second-order synaptic-filter dynamics maps these components to two linearly independent initial-state directions whenever their preparation delays differ.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\mathbf{NB\!-\!A}}
\]

**Natural `B` with `rank(B) >= 2` exists.**

The first CMC-CORE pilot is **not stopped by the admissible-input-geometry gate**. This is not yet permission to perform CORE optimization; the pulse protocol, operating point, and complete model must first be frozen in a subsequent preregistration/specification step.

**STOP.**
