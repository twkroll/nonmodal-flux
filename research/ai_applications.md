# Potential AI / machine-learning applications

**Date:** 2026-09-01  
**Status:** exploratory; not part of P1 scope unless a clean independent result emerges.

## Why the framework may transfer

Non-normal transient amplification is already an established phenomenon in recurrent neural networks and computational neuroscience. Existing work studies how stable recurrent connectivity can transiently amplify selected inputs and use those trajectories for computation.

Our additional ingredient would be to replace a generic activation norm by a **task-derived signed bilinear transfer observable**.

For a locally linearized hidden-state dynamics

```math
\dot h = A h,
```

let `M>0` measure admissible perturbation size, while

```math
\mathcal F_Q(h)=h^\dagger Qh
```

measures a specific directed interaction between representation subspaces.

If `h=(h_a,h_b)`, for example,

```math
Q=\frac12
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & C\\
C^\dagger & 0
\end{bmatrix}
```

produces

```math
h^\dagger Qh = \operatorname{Re}(h_a^\dagger C h_b).
```

With a skew-Hermitian off-diagonal choice, the signed form can instead encode a phase-like directed coupling. This is mathematically analogous to a flux/cross-phase observable rather than an activation-energy norm.

## Application hypothesis A1 — Task-specific transient vulnerability of stable RNNs

Question:

> Which bounded perturbation to a stable recurrent network causes the largest finite-time transfer into a harmful or task-relevant representation channel, even when total hidden-state energy does not grow strongly?

Possible use:

- adversarial robustness of RNNs / state-space sequence models;
- diagnosing transient feature leakage into an unsafe output subspace;
- distinguishing generic activation amplification from task-specific directed transfer.

The key novelty would have to be a principled construction of `Q` from a measurable representation interaction, not an arbitrary weighted sum of hidden units.

## Application hypothesis A2 — Mechanistic interpretability via transfer-optimal perturbations

Partition hidden state into interpretable subspaces (for example, two learned concept subspaces or modules). Construct a bilinear observable measuring directed co-activation/transfer between them and optimize the initial perturbation that maximizes cumulative signed transfer.

Potential output:

- a finite-horizon "most transfer-inducing" perturbation;
- comparison with the usual maximum-activation/singular-vector perturbation;
- a transport-neutral initialization constraint to ensure the coupling is generated by network dynamics rather than inserted at time zero.

This could provide a dynamical-systems complement to static representation similarity methods.

## Application hypothesis A3 — Neural ODE and continuous-depth robustness

Neural ODEs make the transfer particularly direct because their hidden representation follows a continuous-time dynamical system. Around a trajectory,

```math
\delta\dot h = J_f(h(t),t)\,\delta h,
```

so the project naturally generalizes to a time-dependent propagator and `Q(t)`.

Possible question:

> Can a Neural ODE be spectrally/local-stability benign while a task-specific signed representation-transfer observable has a large finite-horizon gain?

This may reveal vulnerabilities missed by Jacobian-norm or activation-norm regularization.

## Application hypothesis A4 — Learned state-space models

Modern sequence models can contain stable or constrained linear state-space dynamics. A signed output Gramian could diagnose which bounded input-state perturbations maximize transfer between latent channels over a finite horizon.

This is especially plausible when model designers explicitly constrain spectra for stability: spectral stability alone does not control every finite-time task-specific observable.

## What would make an AI paper convincing

A separate AI paper would need more than applying the same eigensolver to a trained RNN. At minimum:

1. a **non-ad-hoc construction of the signed observable `Q`** from a task or representation mechanism;
2. evidence that conventional activation/energy gain misses the relevant failure or computation;
3. a transport-neutral or zero-initial-interaction control;
4. tests on at least one RNN/state-space model and one Neural-ODE/continuous-depth setting;
5. a practical outcome such as improved robustness diagnosis, interpretability, architecture design, or regularization.

## Literature relation

- Non-normal RNNs have been proposed explicitly to exploit transient dynamics while maintaining spectral constraints (Kerg et al., 2019).
- Work on transient trajectories in recurrent networks shows that non-normal connectivity can map selected inputs into amplified, computationally useful trajectories.
- Recent reviews of Neural ODEs emphasize stability, robustness, adjoint sensitivity, reachability and explainability as active issues.

Thus **nonnormality in AI is not new**. The potentially fresh part is the Lülff-style move from "how large does the hidden state get?" to "which physically/task-defined signed interaction or transfer becomes large?"
