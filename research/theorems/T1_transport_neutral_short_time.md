# T1 — Short-time generation of signed transport from a transport-neutral input space

**Status:** proved in finite dimension; novelty not established  
**Role:** supporting proposition / diagnostic theorem candidate, not yet a headline novelty claim  
**Date:** 2026-09-01

## Setup

Let

```math
\dot x = A x, \qquad x(0)=Bu,
```

with `A \in \mathbb C^{n\times n}`, `B \in \mathbb C^{n\times m}`. Let the input cost be

```math
E_{in}(u)=u^\dagger R_{in}u, \qquad R_{in}=R_{in}^\dagger\succ0,
```

and let the physical signed transport observable be

```math
\mathcal F_Q(x)=x^\dagger Qx, \qquad Q=Q^\dagger,
```

where `Q` may be indefinite.

Define the cumulative signed transport over `[0,T]` by

```math
\mathcal T_Q(T;u)
=\int_0^T x(t)^\dagger Qx(t)\,dt
=u^\dagger B^\dagger P_Q(T)Bu,
```

with

```math
P_Q(T)=\int_0^T e^{A^\dagger t}Qe^{At}\,dt.
```

Introduce the whitened input-space transport operator

```math
H_Q(T)
=R_{in}^{-1/2}B^\dagger P_Q(T)BR_{in}^{-1/2}.
```

The positive and negative signed cumulative gains are

```math
\mathcal G_{Q,+}(T)=\lambda_{\max}(H_Q(T)),
\qquad
\mathcal G_{Q,-}(T)=\lambda_{\min}(H_Q(T)).
```

The admissible input space is called **transport-neutral at the initial instant** if

```math
B^\dagger QB=0.
```

This is stronger than requiring one selected initial condition to have zero transport: it requires every admissible initial disturbance `x_0=Bu` to satisfy `x_0^\dagger Qx_0=0`.

---

## Proposition T1

Assume `B^\dagger QB=0`. Define

```math
H_1
=R_{in}^{-1/2}
B^\dagger(A^\dagger Q+QA)B
R_{in}^{-1/2}.
```

Then, as `T\downarrow0`,

```math
H_Q(T)=\frac{T^2}{2}H_1+O(T^3)
```

in any matrix norm. Consequently,

```math
\mathcal G_{Q,+}(T)
=\frac{T^2}{2}\lambda_{\max}(H_1)+O(T^3),
```

and

```math
\mathcal G_{Q,-}(T)
=\frac{T^2}{2}\lambda_{\min}(H_1)+O(T^3).
```

In particular, if `\lambda_{\max}(H_1)>0`, then sufficiently small positive horizons admit dynamically generated positive cumulative transport even though every admissible initial state has zero instantaneous transport. If `\lambda_{\min}(H_1)<0`, the analogous statement holds for negative transport.

---

## Proof

For finite-dimensional `A`, the matrix exponential is analytic. Expanding both factors,

```math
e^{A^\dagger t}Qe^{At}
=Q+t(A^\dagger Q+QA)
+\frac{t^2}{2}\left((A^\dagger)^2Q+2A^\dagger QA+QA^2\right)
+O(t^3).
```

Integrating term by term from `0` to `T` gives

```math
P_Q(T)
=TQ
+\frac{T^2}{2}(A^\dagger Q+QA)
+\frac{T^3}{6}\left((A^\dagger)^2Q+2A^\dagger QA+QA^2\right)
+O(T^4).
```

Premultiplication by `B^\dagger`, postmultiplication by `B`, and the transport-neutrality condition `B^\dagger QB=0` yield

```math
B^\dagger P_Q(T)B
=\frac{T^2}{2}B^\dagger(A^\dagger Q+QA)B+O(T^3).
```

Whitening with the fixed nonsingular matrix `R_{in}^{-1/2}` preserves the order of the remainder, hence

```math
H_Q(T)=\frac{T^2}{2}H_1+O(T^3).
```

Both `H_Q(T)` and `H_1` are Hermitian. By Weyl's eigenvalue perturbation inequality,

```math
|\lambda_j(H_Q(T))-\lambda_j(T^2H_1/2)|
\le \|H_Q(T)-T^2H_1/2\|_2
=O(T^3)
```

for every ordered eigenvalue. Taking the largest and smallest eigenvalues proves the two gain expansions. The sign conclusions follow because the `T^2` coefficient dominates the `O(T^3)` remainder for sufficiently small positive `T`. `\square`

---

## Instantaneous version

The same mechanism appears before time integration. For `x_0=Bu`,

```math
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal F_Q(x(t))\bigg|_{t=0}
=u^\dagger B^\dagger(A^\dagger Q+QA)Bu.
```

Thus, on a transport-neutral admissible space, `A^\dagger Q+QA` determines the first instantaneous production of the signed observable, while its time integral begins one order later, at `O(T^2)`.

This distinction matters physically:

- `B^\dagger QB=0` removes transport inserted directly at `t=0`;
- `B^\dagger(A^\dagger Q+QA)B` measures transport generated by the dynamics from that neutral space.

---

## Coordinate invariance check

Under an invertible physical state-coordinate transformation

```math
x'=Sx,
```

with

```math
A'=SAS^{-1},\qquad
Q'=S^{-\dagger}QS^{-1},\qquad
B'=SB,
```

we have exactly

```math
B'^\dagger Q'B'=B^\dagger QB
```

and

```math
B'^\dagger(A'^\dagger Q'+Q'A')B'
=B^\dagger(A^\dagger Q+QA)B.
```

Therefore both transport-neutrality and the leading short-time input-space operator are coordinate invariant when the physical observable is transformed covariantly.

## What T1 does and does not establish

### It establishes

1. a precise nontriviality filter for admissible initial conditions;
2. the leading operator responsible for dynamically generated signed transport;
3. short-time positive and negative gain criteria;
4. a clean analytic target for JAX tests;
5. a coordinate-invariant statement in the physical state geometry.

### It does not establish

1. that the result is novel in abstract linear-systems mathematics;
2. that nonnormality is necessary — `H_1` can be nonzero even for some normal `A` depending on `Q` and `B`;
3. that the effect persists to physically relevant finite horizons;
4. that the transport-optimal differs from the energy-optimal;
5. that a particular plasma input space satisfying `B^\dagger QB=0` is physically admissible;
6. that `H_1` alone determines the optimal direction if its relevant extremal eigenvalue is degenerate or zero. The gain expansion remains valid, but eigenvector selection may require higher order.

---

## Degenerate / higher-order case

If

```math
B^\dagger QB=0
```

and also

```math
B^\dagger(A^\dagger Q+QA)B=0,
```

then cumulative transport begins at order `T^3` or higher. The next coefficient is

```math
\frac{1}{6}B^\dagger
\left((A^\dagger)^2Q+2A^\dagger QA+QA^2\right)B.
```

This suggests a hierarchy of **transport-generation order** for admissible subspaces. Developing that hierarchy into a useful structural theorem is potentially more interesting than T1 alone.

## Prior-art assessment as of 2026-09-01

The targeted search found several adjacent results but no direct match for the full combination

> signed/indefinite physical quadratic transport + autonomous initial-condition optimization + a whole admissible subspace with zero initial transport + finite-horizon signed extremals.

However, the algebra behind T1 is close to established ideas:

- classical nonmodal theory derives short-time energy growth from the Hermitian part of the generator; in a general physical energy metric this is the special case obtained when the observable is the energy form;
- quadratic-output systems with nonzero initial conditions are established in systems/model-reduction literature;
- restricted/semi-norm output optimization is established;
- indefinite quadratic performance indices are established in control, although typically in different forced-control/game formulations.

**Current conclusion:** treat T1 as a rigorous supporting proposition and diagnostic, **not** as the main novelty theorem of P1. A stronger candidate is a hierarchy/bound that exploits the transport-null input space together with a physical free-energy balance, or a plasma-specific theorem showing robust mismatch between free-energy and flux optimals under this restriction.
