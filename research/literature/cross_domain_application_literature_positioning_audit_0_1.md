## 2.2 Strongest Neuro prior-art overlaps

### 2.2.1 Non-normal/transient neural dynamics

Hennequin–Vogels–Gerstner and Bondanelli–Ostojic substantially demote any statement that transient amplification, optimal transient directions, or input/output trajectory geometry is itself new in neuroscience.

The frozen Neuro result must therefore **not** be sold as the first neural demonstration of nonmodal or transiently optimized behavior.

### 2.2.2 DCM and optimal experimental design

Friston et al. establish designed deterministic inputs and effective connectivity as core DCM concepts. Daunizeau et al. explicitly optimize DCM experimental design. Thus a statement such as “DCM has not previously been optimized with respect to stimulation/input design” is false or at least untenable.

The relevant distinction is objective and geometry:

- prior DCM optimal-design work optimizes an **inferential criterion** such as model discrimination;
- the frozen pilot optimizes only the amplitudes of **two already fixed afferent preparation pulses**, then evolves autonomously;
- the two compared criteria are **terminal synaptic-filter storage** and **cumulative contribution of a pre-defined physiological pathway**.

### 2.2.3 Neural-mass optimal control

Salfenmoser–Obermayer establishes nonlinear neural-mass optimal control with time-dependent inputs and state/control costs. This is materially different from the frozen preparation problem, but it removes any broad novelty claim about “optimization of stimulation in neural-mass models.”

### 2.2.4 Recent optimal perturbation design

Ogino et al. (2026) is a particularly important current neighbor because it explicitly optimizes perturbation inputs under a linear dynamical model for neuroscience. It strengthens the need to phrase the frozen result narrowly: the distinction is not “optimal perturbations versus no optimal perturbations,” but **which physical finite-time objective is optimized within a fixed admissible preparation coordinate system**.

## 3.2 Strongest Climate/Ocean prior-art overlaps

### 3.2.1 Stable/damped baroclinic transient growth is classical

Farrell (1985) is essentially a direct prior-art hit for the **background phenomenon** that a damped baroclinic system can exhibit strong finite-time growth while all normal modes decay.

Therefore the frozen Climate pilot must not claim novelty for

\[
\alpha(A_K)<0,
\qquad G_E(T)>1.
\]

That is a classical generalized-stability/nonmodal result in baroclinic dynamics.

### 3.2.2 Heat flux and transient baroclinic dynamics are already linked

Farrell & Ioannou (1994) explicitly formulate a theory for both the statistical energy spectrum and **heat flux** produced by transient baroclinic waves. This substantially demotes any broad statement that adding heat transport to nonmodal baroclinic analysis is new.

The frozen pilot differs because it treats heat transport as a **signed cumulative deterministic finite-time objective optimized over initial conditions**, not as a statistical-equilibrium flux generated under stochastic forcing.

### 3.2.3 Objective/norm-dependent singular vectors are standard

Kim & Morgan, Kuang, Errico et al. and the broader singular-vector literature establish that changing the norm or final metric can change the leading optimal perturbations, sometimes strongly.

Consequently, neither

> “different objectives select different optimal perturbations”

nor

> “the leading modal support can switch when the objective changes”

is by itself a novelty claim.

### 3.2.4 Heat-transport-optimal perturbations already exist in ocean literature

Sévellec et al. (2008) is the strongest direct warning against a broad claim. Their asymptotically stable ocean model has separately defined optimal initial perturbations for overturning and meridional heat transport.

Thus “optimal initial perturbations for heat transport in a stable ocean system” is established prior art.

What remains different in the frozen Climate pilot is the exact comparison:

\[
\text{QG perturbation-energy optimum}
\quad\text{vs}\quad
\text{positive signed cumulative eddy-heat-transport optimum},
\]

computed in the **same damped two-layer Phillips-QG tangent system** with a fully signed positive/negative heat operator and a frozen resolution ladder.
